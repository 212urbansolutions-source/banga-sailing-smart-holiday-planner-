Provisioning guide

How to stock up for a sailing holiday

A good provisioning plan does not need to be complicated. It should keep the crew hydrated, make the first evening easy, leave room for local food discoveries, and avoid filling every locker with things nobody will eat.

Quick list

  • Plan the first 24 hours before anything else.
  • Buy more water than you think you need.
  • Keep breakfasts and lunches simple.
  • Leave space for tavernas and local markets.
  • Pack snacks where the crew can reach them.

The best sailing holiday food plan starts with rhythm. Most crews eat breakfast on board, prepare simple lunches at anchor, and go ashore for several dinners. That means you do not need a floating supermarket. You need a calm first day, flexible basics, and enough drinks to keep everyone comfortable in the sun.

Start with the first day

Arrival day is usually the messiest part of the trip. People are checking in, unpacking, listening to the technical briefing, and finding their cabins. Prepare for that by buying an easy first dinner, breakfast for the next morning, coffee, drinking water, ice, and snacks. If the crew can relax on board without another supermarket run, the holiday starts better.

Water is the real priority

In warm cruising areas, water disappears quickly. Plan drinking water separately from cooking water, and keep a few bottles chilled for the cockpit. Soft drinks, sparkling water, and juice are useful, but plain water is the item you will miss fastest if you underbuy.

Build meals around simple combinations

Choose food that works in several ways: pasta, rice, couscous, eggs, cheese, tomatoes, tuna, olives, bread, fruit, yoghurt, nuts, and good olive oil. These ingredients can become breakfast, lunch, or a quick dinner without turning the galley into a restaurant kitchen. For one-week charters, two or three reliable on-board dinners are usually enough if you also plan to eat ashore.

Think about storage before you buy

Boat fridges are smaller than home fridges, and locker space is awkward. Avoid fragile packaging, large cardboard boxes, and food that needs perfect refrigeration. Remove excess packaging before stowing, keep heavy items low, and separate breakfast, snacks, drinks, and dinner ingredients so the crew can find things without unpacking the boat.

Do not forget the small comforts

Coffee, tea, salt, pepper, oil, washing-up liquid, bin bags, kitchen roll, sunscreen, and basic medicine are easy to overlook. They are not glamorous, but they make the boat feel organised. If children are on board, add familiar snacks and a few no-cooking options for hungry moments after swimming.

Leave room for the destination

A sailing holiday should not be planned like a bunker. Buy enough to be comfortable, then let the route add flavour: fish from a harbour market, bread from a village bakery, fruit from a roadside stall, or dinner at a family taverna. The best provisioning plan supports the trip without taking over the trip.
